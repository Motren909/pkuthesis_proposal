\begin{cabstract}
随着具身智能与泛在智能物体逐步进入生产和生活环境，人机关系正由人使用单一设备完成离散任务，转向人与多个具身智能体共享物理情境、持续感知彼此状态并动态协作的人机共生。在这一过程中，交互对象、任务进程与环境状态均可能实时变化，用户因而不仅需要“操作设备”，还需要在多个共处对象之间迅速辨认当前目标、自然地表达交互意图，并将指令准确地作用于特定对象。以协同工厂为例，操作人员可能需要在机械臂、移动机器人和智能工位之间连续切换，查看某一对象的状态并随即下达指令，而不应反复退出当前任务以搜索设备。由此，面向多对象的、符合人类空间认知习惯的直观定向交互，成为人机共生时代的新需求。

然而，现有交互方式难以满足这一需求。二维码扫描、设备列表检索和无线配对等方式忽视了指向、朝向与注视中已经包含的空间关注信息；即使用户已明确关注某一对象，仍需在界面中重新查找、确认并连接设备，使物理对象选择、数字身份识别、连接建立与动作执行相互割裂。究其根因，现有系统无法将人的自然注意力线索直接转化为可靠的对象绑定，只能借助额外的显式步骤恢复交互过程中丢失的注意力信息，因而增加了多对象切换的时间、操作与认知负担。因此，如何捕捉并利用可测量的注意力线索，在不增加额外操作的前提下准确绑定交互对象，是实现直观定向交互的核心问题。
本文围绕\textbf{如何挖掘、利用人真实注意力并可靠地转化为高效、精准的定向交互}这一问题展开研究。通过对人类注意力信息由浅入深的挖掘和利用，形成\textbf{“所指即所控—所看即所控—所思即所控”}的三种定向交互模式，旨在为未来人机共生提供一个新的交互模式解决方案。具体来说，主要研究工作如下：

（1）针对广泛普及的手机等手持终端，本文提出基于指向的非接触定向交互系统 RetroAR。本文首先发现，手机指向所携带的用户位置和方向信息可由可见光的定向传播特性自然保留。基于此，设计了面向商用手机的可见光定向交互机制：用户以手机闪光灯照射逆反射标签，标签调制反射光并由手机后置摄像头接收，无须检索和连接无线设备。为兼顾数据通信与空间锚定，本文进一步设计动态光标签 ViTag，并结合消息级动态兴趣区、快速凸包检测和自适应时空混合编解码，使系统在手机运动、视角变化和复杂光学背景下稳定读取标签并估计六自由度位姿。系统评估表明，RetroAR 最远可在约 $4\,\mathrm{m}$ 距离和约 $100^\circ$ 视角下工作；用户研究表明，其交互开销相比传统Wi-Fi扫码方案降低50\%，且交互负担更小。RetroAR 验证了光学链路利用“指向”提升定向交互效率的可行性，为“所指即所控”提供系统依据；

（2）针对快速普及的AR眼镜终端，本文提出基于互补红外光学通信的定向交互系统 EchoSight。本文发现，相较于需要主动抬起并对准的手持设备，眼镜方向能够自然贴近用户的观察方向，而红外光束的定向特性可以保留用户注意力指向性，使目标识别与控制一步完成：用户看向目标并做出手势后，眼镜端 SightReader 发射调制红外光，对象端 EchoTag 接收指令并调制反射光，回送身份与状态信息，无须预先注册或建立无线连接。本文进一步设计双路互补光学前端，结合双波段差分信号、对准检测和按需唤醒机制，抑制环境光干扰与非目标响应，实现低时延双向通信。系统评估表明，EchoSight 最远可在约 $5\,\mathrm{m}$ 距离和约 $120^\circ$ 视角下工作；用户研究表明，其交互效率相比二维码扫描和语音控制方案至少提升5倍，且主观负担更低、交互流畅性更好。EchoSight 验证了“视线”比“指向”承载更直观的注意力信息，为“所看即所控”提供系统依据；

（3）针对未来小型化、多模态的新式穿戴设备，本文拟提出利用眼动捕获注意力并结合脑电解码识别意图的定向交互系统 Omnisight。本文首先观察到，眼镜光轴与用户实际注视点之间仍可能存在偏差，限制了注意力线索的准确获取；基于此，设计了由眼动驱动的“物理空间三维鼠标指针”：在AR眼镜上集成基于双目相机的眼动追踪模块，并设计光学云台实现LED波束转向，使光束主动追随用户的真实视线并对准关注目标。与此同时，本文进一步引入基于水凝胶电极的脑电意图识别机制，融合眼动和任务相关脑电信息，区分“看向但不操作”与“主动选择并执行”，以降低仅凭注视触发交互所带来的误操作；Omnisight 的研究旨在探索从“方向近似注意”到“眼动对齐+意图判别”的可行路径，为“所思即所控”提供待验证的系统方案。

\end{cabstract}

\begin{eabstract}
As smart glasses, smart homes, collaborative robots, and ubiquitous intelligent objects enter everyday environments, interaction is shifting from operating a preselected device toward human--machine symbiosis, in which people and multiple agents share physical context and coordinate as tasks evolve. Yet QR-code scanning, device-list search, and wireless pairing often require users to specify a target again after pointing or looking at it. This separation among object discovery, identity verification, connection establishment, and action execution introduces delay as well as attentional and physical burdens. Attention carries cues about which object matters and whether action is being prepared, but no single sensor observes it completely. The fundamental problem is how measurable attention proxies can support reliable object binding and intent confirmation without repeated explicit operations.

This proposal studies \textbf{how progressively more direct cues of human attention can turn spatial focus into reliable and executable directional interaction}. It adopts a pipeline of cue acquisition, object binding, intent confirmation, session maintenance, action, and feedback, and follows a three-level progression of \textbf{pointing, looking, and intending}. The research first uses handheld-device direction for target selection and local optical exchange, then moves the interface to smart glasses for in-situ bidirectional interaction, and finally will use measured gaze to steer the beam and test whether task-related electroencephalography (EEG) adds evidence of operational intent. The three systems examine the externalization, alignment, and discrimination of attention while separating device orientation from gaze and looking from intending. The main components are as follows.

(1) To reduce dependence on list-based search and wireless discovery in mobile augmented reality, this work developed RetroAR, a contactless directional interaction system based on smartphone pointing. A user illuminates a visible-light retroreflective tag, ViTag, with the phone flashlight. A liquid-crystal matrix modulates the reflected light, which is captured by the rear camera. Beam direction and camera field of view jointly constrain candidate objects, allowing the act of pointing to perform both target discovery and local data exchange. To remain usable under phone motion, viewpoint changes, and background interference, the system combines a dynamic region of interest, geometric tag detection, adaptive spatiotemporal hybrid decoding, and six-degree-of-freedom pose estimation. Reported experiments demonstrate an operating distance of approximately $4\,\mathrm{m}$ and an angular range of approximately $100^\circ$. Translation and rotation errors in pose estimation are as low as approximately $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $4.7^\circ$, respectively. In the evaluated contactless-control tasks, interaction time was reduced by more than twofold relative to the Wi-Fi baseline. These results support the feasibility of preserving spatial selection through a directional optical link. RetroAR nevertheless measures phone orientation rather than gaze and still requires the user to raise and maintain alignment of a handheld device.

(2) To reduce the physical burden of handheld alignment, this work developed EchoSight, an in-situ bidirectional interaction system for smart glasses. A glasses-mounted SightReader and an object-mounted EchoTag form a near-infrared optical link. A dual-element optical design and alignment detection exploit spatial directionality to suppress responses from non-target objects, enabling a user to obtain identity and status information and transmit control data while observing an object. The design places object selection, identity acquisition, and information exchange in the same situated channel, reducing preregistration, menu search, and additional connection steps. Bidirectional communication further extends interaction from tag reading to control of physical objects. Reported experiments demonstrate room-scale communication at distances up to approximately $5\,\mathrm{m}$ and viewing angles up to approximately $120^\circ$. A user study with 12 participants found, under the evaluated tasks and baselines, at least a fivefold reduction in interaction latency compared with QR-code scanning and voice control, together with lower subjective workload. These results support the efficiency and experiential benefits of wearable directional interaction in the studied tasks. However, the glasses-mounted optical axis only approximates viewing direction: it neither measures the actual point of gaze nor distinguishes casual observation from an intention to act. Head turns and occlusion may also interrupt an all-optical session.

(3) To address divergence between device direction and gaze, ambiguity between gaze and operational intent, and the fragility of persistent optical links, this proposal will develop Omnisight for attention alignment and intent discrimination. First, the glasses will measure gaze, calibrate transformations among eye coordinates, head pose, and beam coordinates, and drive a steerable optical transmitter so that the beam actively follows the attended target. Fixed and steerable optics will be compared across target distances, target spacing, head motion, and occlusion using alignment error, target-selection error, latency, power consumption, and wearing burden. Second, gaze and gel-electrode EEG will be collected synchronously in repeatable tasks that distinguish passive looking, active selection, and action confirmation. Gaze-only, EEG-only, multimodal fusion, and gaze-plus-explicit-confirmation methods will be evaluated through false activations, misses, precision, recall, and decision latency. EEG will be retained only if its incremental value justifies its acquisition and wearing costs. Third, directional light will perform target discovery and identity confirmation, after which the authorized physical object will be bound to a Bluetooth identity for feedback and persistent communication. Object identifiers, state transitions, timeouts, switching, and rebinding will be designed to prevent mismatch between the selected object and controlled device. Binding errors, session recovery time, end-to-end latency, and energy will be measured under stationary, mobile, head-turning, and occluded conditions. These capabilities remain proposed rather than demonstrated and will be established through prototypes, controlled comparisons, and complete-task user studies.

Together, the systems move from handheld pointing and glasses orientation toward measured gaze and physiological evidence, narrowing the gap between observable cues, attention, and readiness to act. Target-selection errors, false activations and misses, latency, recovery under motion and occlusion, energy, wearing burden, and subjective experience will jointly characterize the benefits, costs, and boundaries of attention-driven directional interaction, providing a system-level basis for natural, accurate, and low-burden collaboration with multiple intelligent agents.
\end{eabstract}
